About the Student Success Tool

Beginning in 2027–2028, the Student Success Tool (SST) replaces STAAR in Texas with beginning-, middle-, and end-of-year assessments. TEA’s draft elementary science blueprints organize questions around four strands — Matter and Energy; Force, Motion, and Energy; Earth and Space; and Organisms and Environments — using multiple choice, multiselect, multipart, and inline choice formats.

Formats change; the thinking underneath does not. A student who can only recall a definition stalls the moment a question changes one condition. The activities here are designed around that gap:

  • Articulate — explain what happened in your own words.
  • Connect — tie the evidence to the underlying concept.
  • Extend — predict what changes when one condition changes.

Score what students say with a five-level SOLO guide, then teach from the level they actually show you. Full details on the assessment design are in TEA’s House Bill 8 student assessment resources.
