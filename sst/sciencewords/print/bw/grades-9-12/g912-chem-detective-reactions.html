Two opposite reactions

A student heats iron filings with sulfur. The two elements combine into a single new compound, iron sulfide — a SYNTHESIS. Later she heats a small amount of hydrogen peroxide, and it breaks apart into water and oxygen gas, one compound becoming two simpler substances — a DECOMPOSITION. One reaction builds up; the other takes apart. The word parts name the direction.
