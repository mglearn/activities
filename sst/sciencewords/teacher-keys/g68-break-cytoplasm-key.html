Break It: Cytoplasm

Grades 6–8 · Organisms and Environments · BOY · Articulate · Break It · 10 min

Teacher key

  • CYTOPLASM
    CYTO = cell; PLASM = formed material; the jelly-like material that fills a cell.
    Both parts are needed; a student who stops at 'cell' has only half.
  • CYTOLOGY
    CYTO = cell; LOGY = the study of; the study of cells.
  • What do both words have to do with?
    Both are about cells. CYTO names the cell; the second part says what about it — the material, or the study.
    This is the generalization the activity is built to produce.
  • Scientist studying diseased cells — which word?
    Cytology. The work is the study of cells, which is what LOGY signals.
    Look for the reason, not just the word.

What to look for

Students should use CYTO to justify both answers. A student who cannot say what CYTO means is at level 1.

Response evidence

1 — One clueIdentifies one relevant word part.
2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
3 — Connected meaningCombines word structure and science context.
4 — TransferUses known word parts successfully in a new situation.
