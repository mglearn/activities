Sort It: Herbivore, Carnivore, Insectivore

Grades 3–5 · Organisms and Environments · MOY · Connect · Sort It · 15 min

Teacher key

  • HERBIVORE column
    grasshopper eating grass; cow eating hay; rabbit eating clover.
  • CARNIVORE column
    snake eating a frog; hawk eating a mouse.
  • INSECTIVORE column
    bat catching moths; anteater eating ants; frog catching flies.
    Insectivores are a kind of carnivore, so students who place these in the carnivore column are not wrong — ask them to explain.
  • Which animal in the chain is a herbivore?
    The grasshopper. HERB means plant, and the grasshopper eats grass.
  • Is the frog a carnivore, an insectivore, or both?
    Both. An insectivore eats insects, and insects are animals, so an insectivore is a kind of carnivore.
    This is the reasoning item on the page. Accept either single label if the explanation is sound; the strongest answer says both and explains the overlap.
  • If grasshoppers disappeared?
    Frogs would have less food, so the frog population would likely fall, and snakes would then have less food too.
    Look for effects moving along the chain, not just one step.
  • ACE Extend: omnivore
    An animal that eats both plants and animals. Students may name bears, raccoons, or people.

What to look for

The frog item separates level 2 from level 3. A level 3 response explains the overlap between the two categories rather than choosing one.

Response evidence

1 — One clueIdentifies one relevant word part.
2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
3 — Connected meaningCombines word structure and science context.
4 — TransferUses known word parts successfully in a new situation.
