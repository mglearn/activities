Mystery Science Word (EOY): Reading a Formula's Name

Grades 9–12 · Chemistry · EOY · Extend · Mystery Word · 20 min

Teacher key

  • DINITROGEN TETROXIDE
    DI = two nitrogen, TETR(A) = four oxygen, -IDE = two-element compound; two nitrogen and four oxygen atoms (N₂O₄).
  • PHOSPHORUS PENTACHLORIDE
    PENTA = five chlorine, -IDE = two-element compound; one phosphorus with five chlorine (PCl₅).
  • POTASSIUM CHLORATE
    -ATE = an oxygen-rich ion, so it contains oxygen (chlorate is ClO₃⁻); potassium plus that ion.
    Reward reading the -ATE as 'contains oxygen' without a chart.
  • SODIUM SULFIDE
    -IDE = two elements, no oxygen signalled; sodium plus sulfur (Na₂S).
  • How prefixes and endings let you read a new name
    The number prefix counts the atoms and the ending names the kind of ion, so the two rules together decode a name never seen before.
    This is the core end-of-year transfer skill.
  • Dinitrogen tetroxide count
    Two nitrogen and four oxygen — from DI (two) and TETRA (four).
    Correct choice is the first option.
  • Carbon tetrachloride
    TETRA signals four chlorine atoms (CCl₄).
    The transfer item: a brand-new name read by its prefix.

What to look for

The end-of-year target is combining two rules to read an unstudied name. A student who needs to be told the formula first has not yet trusted the prefix and the ending.

Response evidence

1 — One clueIdentifies one relevant word part.
2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
3 — Connected meaningCombines word structure and science context.
4 — TransferUses known word parts successfully in a new situation.
