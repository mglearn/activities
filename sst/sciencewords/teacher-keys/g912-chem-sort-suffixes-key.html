Sort It: -ide, -ate, or -ite

Grades 9–12 · Chemistry · MOY · Connect · Sort It · 15 min

Teacher key

  • -IDE column
    chloride, sulfide, oxide — names of two-element compounds.
    These carry no oxygen implied by the ending.
  • -ATE column
    sulfate, nitrate, carbonate — oxygen ions, the richer form.
  • -ITE column
    sulfite, nitrite — oxygen ions, the poorer form.
  • Sulfate vs sulfite oxygen
    The -ATE form has more oxygen (SO₄²⁻, four) and the -ITE form has less (SO₃²⁻, three), so the ending predicts the count order.
    Require the ending tied to the oxygen count, with the formulas if possible.
  • A name ending in -IDE
    It is a compound of just two elements, with no oxygen signalled by the ending.
    Reward 'two elements' from the ending alone.
  • Phosphide
    No oxygen is signalled — the -IDE ending marks a two-element compound.
    Transfer of the -IDE rule to an unseen name.

What to look for

The key behaviour is reading the ending as a clue to composition. A student who sorts by the element name rather than the ending has missed the point.

Response evidence

1 — One clueIdentifies one relevant word part.
2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
3 — Connected meaningCombines word structure and science context.
4 — TransferUses known word parts successfully in a new situation.
