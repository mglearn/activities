Mystery Science Word (EOY): -ase and -ose

Grades 9–12 · Biology · EOY · Extend · Mystery Word · 20 min

Teacher key

  • SUCROSE
    -OSE, so a sugar. The passage says it is broken into smaller sugars.
  • SUCRASE
    -ASE, so an enzyme. It does the breaking.
  • MALTOSE / MALTASE
    Maltose is the sugar (-OSE); maltase is the enzyme (-ASE) that breaks it.
    Reward students who apply the endings without extra information.
  • Enzyme vs sugar from the ending alone
    The -ASE ending marks the enzyme and the -OSE ending marks the sugar, so the last three letters settle it before anything else is known.
    This is the core transfer skill of the task.
  • LACTASE on a label
    An enzyme — the -ASE ending. LACTOSE, ending in -OSE, would be the sugar it breaks.
    Correct choice is 'an enzyme'; reward the paired contrast with lactose.
  • FRUCTOSE and PROTEASE
    Fructose is a sugar (-OSE); protease is an enzyme (-ASE).
    The end-of-year transfer item: two brand-new words settled by the endings alone.

What to look for

The end-of-year target is transfer: using -ASE and -OSE to classify words never taught. A student who needs to know the molecule first has not yet trusted the morphology.

Response evidence

1 — One clueIdentifies one relevant word part.
2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
3 — Connected meaningCombines word structure and science context.
4 — TransferUses known word parts successfully in a new situation.
