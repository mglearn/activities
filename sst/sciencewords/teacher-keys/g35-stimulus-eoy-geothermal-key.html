Decode the Stimulus: Geothermal (EOY)

Grades 3–5 · Earth and Space · EOY · Extend · Decode the Stimulus · 20 min

Teacher key

  • Step 1: Break it
    GEO = Earth; THERM = heat; -AL = relating to.
  • Step 2: Predict
    Anything about heat and Earth. 'Earth heat' is a successful prediction at this stage.
  • Step 4: Final meaning
    Heat that comes from inside Earth, which can be used as an energy resource.
    The diagram supplies the mechanism (water heated by hot rock returns to the surface) and the table supplies the pattern (temperature rises with depth).
  • Multiple choice
    Option A — pump water down to hot rock and bring the heated water back up.
    The table shows 95 °C at 1,500 meters, and the diagram shows water circulating down and back up. Option B relies on the Sun, not Earth's heat; option C is far too shallow to reach hot rock; option D is not an energy plan.
  • Evidence for the choice
    Temperature increases with depth: 18 °C at the surface, 40 °C at 500 m, 95 °C at 1,500 m.
    Require a number or the trend from the table, not just 'it is hot down there.'
  • Step 5: RENEWABLE
    A resource that is replaced naturally in a short time. Earth's interior heat and moving water both keep being resupplied, so neither runs out the way coal does.
    Accept answers that focus on 'does not run out' if the student explains why.
  • ACE Extend: the 20 °C town
    No. The plan depends on rock that gets much hotter with depth. If every depth is 20 °C, there is no heat source to warm the water.
    This is the transfer item. A level 4 response uses the temperature evidence to rule the plan out rather than repeating the definition.

What to look for

This sheet is the end-of-year transfer task. The distinguishing behavior is using the table and the diagram together with the word parts. A student who answers from the word parts alone cannot rule out the 20 °C town.
