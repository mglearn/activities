Break It: Chromosome and Cell Bodies

Grades 9–12 · Biology · BOY · Articulate · Break It · 10 min

Teacher key

  • CHROMOSOME
    CHROMO = colour, SOME = body; a 'coloured body' — a coiled strand of DNA that carries genes.
    Accept 'DNA structure that carries genes' once both parts are named.
  • RIBOSOME
    RIBO = ribose, SOME = body; the cell body, built partly of RNA, that assembles proteins.
  • What do the two share?
    Both are bodies — structures inside the cell. SOME marks each as a distinct cell body.
    This is the generalization the break is built to produce.
  • Where the chromosome name came from
    From CHROMO, colour: chromosomes readily absorb coloured dyes under the microscope, so early scientists named them coloured bodies.
    Reward students who separate 'named for taking up dye' from 'colourful in life'.
  • LYSOSOME prediction
    A cell body that breaks things down — LYSIS means breaking apart, SOME means body.
    Transfer of the SOME root plus the known LYSIS.

What to look for

Look for SOME used to justify 'a cell body' in each case. A student who cannot separate the shared root from the first part is at level 2.

Response evidence

1 — One clueIdentifies one relevant word part.
2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
3 — Connected meaningCombines word structure and science context.
4 — TransferUses known word parts successfully in a new situation.
