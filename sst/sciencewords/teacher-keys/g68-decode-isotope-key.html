Decode It: Isotope

Grades 6–8 · Matter and Energy · MOY · Connect · Decode It · 15 min

Teacher key

  • Break it
    ISO = equal or same; TOPE = place. Same place — on the periodic table.
  • Predict
    Atoms that share the same place (same element) but differ in some way.
    Accept predictions that name 'same element, something different'.
  • Same and different in the table
    Same: 6 protons in all three, so all are carbon. Different: the neutron count (6, 7, 8), which changes the mass. Isotopes are atoms of one element with different neutron numbers.
    Require the student to read the table, not recite a definition.
  • Why still carbon?
    Same number of protons. The element is set by the proton count, not the neutrons.
    Correct answer is the first option.
  • Two oxygen atoms with different neutrons
    Yes, they are isotopes: same protons (8), different neutrons.
    Transfer item — reward reasoning from the proton rule.

What to look for

The transfer behaviour is applying the proton rule to a new element. A student who says 'different mass so different element' has the idea backwards.

Response evidence

2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
