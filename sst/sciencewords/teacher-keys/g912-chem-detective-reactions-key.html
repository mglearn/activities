Word Detective: Reaction Types

Grades 9–12 · Chemistry · MOY · Connect · Word Detective · 20 min

Teacher key

  • SYNTHESIS
    SYN = together, THESIS = putting; a reaction that combines reactants into one product. Evidence: iron and sulfur combine into iron sulfide.
    Require the evidence column to cite the combining in the passage.
  • DECOMPOSITION
    DE = reversal/taking apart, COMPOSITION = putting together; one compound breaks into simpler substances. Evidence: hydrogen peroxide breaks into water and oxygen.
  • SYN- vs DE-
    SYN- means building up (putting together) and DE- means taking apart, so the prefixes name the opposite directions of the two reactions.
    Reward the opposite-direction contrast tied to the prefixes.
  • A compound split into simpler parts
    Decomposition — the DE- prefix signals the taking apart.
    Require the prefix to justify the choice.
  • Hydrogen and oxygen forming water
    Synthesis — two substances combine into one, which SYN- names.
    Transfer using the prefix.

What to look for

The behaviour is reading SYN- and DE- as opposite directions and citing the passage. A student who swaps them has not used the prefixes.

Response evidence

1 — One clueIdentifies one relevant word part.
2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
3 — Connected meaningCombines word structure and science context.
4 — TransferUses known word parts successfully in a new situation.
