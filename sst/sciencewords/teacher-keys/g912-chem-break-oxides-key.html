Break It: Counting Atoms in a Name

Grades 9–12 · Chemistry · BOY · Articulate · Break It · 10 min

Teacher key

  • MONOXIDE
    MONO = one, OX = oxygen, -IDE = two-element compound; a compound with one oxygen. Carbon monoxide is CO.
  • DIOXIDE
    DI = two, OX = oxygen, -IDE = two-element compound; a compound with two oxygens. Carbon dioxide is CO₂.
  • TRIOXIDE
    TRI = three, OX = oxygen, -IDE = two-element compound; a compound with three oxygens.
  • What -IDE tells you
    That the compound is made of just two elements.
    This is the shared generalization across the three words.
  • CO vs CO₂
    Carbon monoxide has one oxygen (MONO); carbon dioxide has two (DI). The prefix is the only difference in the count.
    Require the prefixes to carry the explanation.
  • SULFUR TRIOXIDE
    TRI signals three oxygens, so SO₃. Reasoning: the prefix counts the oxygen atoms.
    Transfer of the number-prefix rule to a formula.

What to look for

Look for the prefix used to state the oxygen count and -IDE used for 'two elements'. A student who cannot separate the count from the ending is at level 2.

Response evidence

1 — One clueIdentifies one relevant word part.
2 — Several cluesIdentifies multiple parts but does not connect them.
3 — Connected meaningCombines word structure and science context.
4 — TransferUses known word parts successfully in a new situation.
