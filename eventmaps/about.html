History Event Maps

iAbout

History Event Maps is a family of visual historical-thinking organizers for grades 3–5, 6–8, and 9–12 social-studies classrooms. Instead of one generic worksheet, each map supports a distinct form of historical reasoning — chronology, cause and effect, perspective, turning points, evidence, systems, and continuity and change.

How it works: Scan → Map → Compare → Expand → Revise → Conclude. Students examine two or three sources, complete what they can on the map, compare their thinking, then revise it as the teacher projects the same organizer and adds historical context.

The site is a static, self-contained project built to run in any modern browser (or offline) with no build step required to use it. It is part of the mglearn Learning Activities Hub.

Standards. Activities align to the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS). Codes are transcribed verbatim from 19 TAC Chapter 113 and shown with a note asking districts to confirm their locally adopted standards — the June 2026 K–8 revision will renumber some codes. TEKS are never fabricated; uncertain items are flagged for review.

Languages. The interface, routine, and every organizer label are available in seven languages: English, Spanish, Vietnamese, Arabic, Simplified Chinese, Urdu, and Hindi, with right-to-left support for Arabic and Urdu. Translated activity content is machine-assisted and pending fluent-speaker review.

🔒Privacy

No logins. No accounts. No student data collected.

This project collects nothing. It requires no login or account, asks for no names or email, uses no classroom identifiers, and includes no third-party trackers or analytics. Nothing students write on a map is stored on a server — organizers are filled in on paper or in the browser and printed.

The only thing stored locally in your browser is a language preference (tcea.breakouts.lang), shared with other mglearn activities so your language choice carries across the site. Clearing your browser storage resets it to your browser's language. No preference is ever transmitted.

♿Accessibility

History Event Maps targets WCAG 2.1 AA. Design commitments:

  • Semantic heading structure and landmark regions.
  • Full keyboard navigation with a visible focus outline.
  • Adequate colour contrast, and no meaning carried by colour alone — the grayscale mode distinguishes information by line weight, border style, glyphs, and icons so it survives black-and-white printing.
  • Correct lang and dir attributes, with right-to-left layout for Arabic and Urdu.
  • Live HTML/SVG text (not images of text) so labels scale, reflow, and read aloud in every language.
  • Labelled controls and organizer regions for assistive technology; decorative icons hidden from it.
  • Mobile-friendly pages with no horizontal scrolling of ordinary content; printable organizers stay usable when enlarged.
  • Reduced-motion support (animations are removed when the reader prefers reduced motion).

Found an accessibility barrier? Please report it through the mglearn Learning Activities Hub so we can fix it.
