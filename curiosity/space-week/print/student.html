1. Mystery Mission

Read the four clues one at a time and try to name the real space mission. The clues get easier as you go, so guess as early as you can.

Mystery 1

  1. In 1969 this mission's lander was nicknamed 'Eagle,' while the command module stayed in orbit as 'Columbia.'
  2. Its crew left a plaque reading 'We came in peace for all mankind' and honored the fallen Apollo 1 crew.
  3. The landing site in the Sea of Tranquility became known as Tranquility Base.
  4. This was the mission where humans first walked on the Moon and said, 'That's one small step for a man, one giant leap for mankind.'

Mystery 2

  1. Launched in 1977, it carries a 12-inch gold-plated copper phonograph disk of Earth's sounds and images.
  2. It flew past Jupiter and Saturn before heading toward the edge of the solar system.
  3. That disk it carries is called the Golden Record, a time capsule for any who might find it.
  4. In August 2012 it became the first spacecraft to reach interstellar space, beyond the Sun's protective bubble.

My answer:

Mystery 3

  1. Launched in 1990, it soon revealed a flaw called spherical aberration that made its early images blurry.
  2. Astronauts visited it five times between 1993 and 2009 to repair and upgrade it in orbit.
  3. It was carried to space aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery on mission STS-31.
  4. This orbiting telescope produced famous 'Deep Field' images showing thousands of distant galaxies.

My answer:

Mystery 4

  1. Launched in 1997, it carried a European probe named Huygens that later parachuted onto a distant moon.
  2. It spent 13 years orbiting a ringed planet and discovered a global ocean on the moon Enceladus.
  3. In 2005 its Huygens probe landed on Titan, the most distant landing ever made in our solar system.
  4. In 2017 it ended its mission with a 'Grand Finale' plunge into Saturn's atmosphere.

My answer:

Mystery 5

  1. Launched in 2006, it took about nine and a half years to reach its first target.
  2. In 2019 it flew past a distant Kuiper Belt object later named Arrokoth.
  3. It sent back the first clear close-up images of a famous dwarf planet.
  4. In July 2015 it became the first spacecraft to fly by Pluto and its moons.

My answer:

Mystery 6

  1. Arriving in 2016, it became the farthest solar-powered spacecraft from Earth.
  2. It carries three 30-foot solar arrays because its destination gets only about 4% of the sunlight Earth receives.
  3. It was the first mission to orbit an outer planet from pole to pole.
  4. It studies Jupiter, the largest planet, diving close to its swirling cloud tops.

My answer:

Mystery 7

  1. It landed on Feb. 18, 2021, in a crater that once held a lake and river delta about 3.5 billion years ago.
  2. It carried a small companion that would attempt something never done before on another world.
  3. It touched down in Jezero Crater to search for signs of ancient microbial life.
  4. This rover delivered the Ingenuity helicopter, which made the first powered flight on another planet.

My answer:

Mystery 8

  1. It uses seven Venus flybys to gradually shrink its orbit, coming closer to its target than any spacecraft before.
  2. At closest approach it travels about 430,000 mph, making it the fastest human-made object.
  3. In 2021 it crossed the Alfven critical surface and flew through the Sun's corona.
  4. It became the first spacecraft to 'touch' the Sun by flying through its upper atmosphere.

My answer:

Mystery 9

  1. It orbits the Sun about a million miles from Earth at a spot called the second Lagrange point (L2).
  2. Its 18 hexagonal mirror segments are coated with a thin layer of gold and folded to fit inside the rocket.
  3. Launched on Dec. 25, 2021, it is the largest, most powerful telescope ever sent to space.
  4. This infrared observatory captured stunning new images of the Pillars of Creation and the earliest galaxies.
