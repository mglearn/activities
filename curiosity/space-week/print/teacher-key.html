3. Scale It

Order, smallest → largest diameter:
  1. Ceres (dwarf planet) — about 940 km
  2. Earth's Moon — about 3,475 km
  3. Mercury — about 4,879 km
  4. Mars — about 6,792 km
  5. Earth — about 12,756 km
  6. Jupiter — about 142,984 km
  7. The Sun — about 1,391,000 km

The Sun's diameter is roughly 109 times Earth's, and about 1.3 million Earths could fit inside its volume.

Diameters: NASA NSSDCA Planetary Fact Sheet; Ceres: NASA Science.

4. Image Detective

Reveal: The Pillars of Creation, captured by NASA's James Webb Space Telescope (NIRCam) in 2022. These columns of cool gas and dust lie within the Eagle Nebula (M16), about 6,500 light-years away, in a region where new stars are actively forming.

Caption: The Pillars of Creation, captured by NASA's James Webb Space Telescope (NIRCam) in 2022. (NASA Science / ESA)

Notice / Wonder have no single right answer — assess the quality of observation and reasoning.

Source: NASA Science, "NASA's Webb Takes Star-Filled Portrait of Pillars of Creation."

5. Mission Patch Studio

Creation task — no answer key. Score against the rubric:

  • Mission has a clear name.
  • Names a real destination in space (moon, planet, asteroid, or region).
  • States one focused scientific question.
  • Includes ≥2 symbols, each explained.
  • Provides a one-sentence design rationale linking the art to the goal.

Example: Mission name: FROST. Destination: Europa, Jupiter's icy moon. Goal: Find out if a salty ocean lies under the ice. Symbols: a cracked ice sheet (Europa's surface) and a water droplet (the hidden ocean). Explanation: The blue-and-white patch shows a droplet breaking through cracked ice to represent the search for a hidden ocean.
