# PlotPoint — Literary Analysis Poster Prompts

Ready-to-paste image-generation prompts for **classroom literary-analysis posters**,
one set per grade level (5 each), anchored to the most widely-taught Texas ELAR
texts at that level. Every poster is in the **PlotPoint house style** (colors matched
to the hub's navy→plum→gold hero gradient) and carries an **ACE badge** (Articulate ·
Connect · Extend) so the poster is a thinking tool, not just décor.

> **How to use:** paste the **Shared Style Block** first, then a poster's **Prompt**
> block, into your image/design generator (or hand them to a designer). Each poster
> also lists its **ACE triad** text — put those three lines in the poster's bottom
> badge. Posters analyze and reference the works; they reproduce **no** copyrighted
> book-cover art and **no** long quotations (callouts are paraphrased). Several posters
> note a **Pairs with** PlotPoint room you can assign alongside.

## Shared Style Block (paste at the top of every prompt)

> Design a print-ready **vertical 18×24 in classroom poster** in the **PlotPoint house
> style**. **Core palette (match exactly):** deep navy `#1C2247` and `#12163A`
> backgrounds; plum `#7A4FCE` and `#5A34A6` accents; bright gold `#F5C542` highlights;
> soft lavender-white `#F5F4FB` text panels. Add **one signature accent color drawn from
> the text's key symbol** (specified per poster) as a focal pop over the navy/plum base.
> A small **gold dot with a soft glow** sits as the PlotPoint mark near the title.
> **Typography:** bold rounded display face for the title (Fredoka-like); clean humanist
> sans for body (Nunito-like); all text crisp, legible, and correctly spelled.
> **Layout, top to bottom:** (1) a bold **title banner** with the work's title + author;
> (2) a large **central symbolic illustration** (original instructional art — no copyrighted
> covers or stills); (3) **2–3 gold-ribboned "analysis callout" tags** naming a theme,
> symbol, or device; (4) a compact **ACE badge** across the bottom: three plum chips
> labeled **A · Articulate**, **C · Connect**, **E · Extend**, each with one short line.
> High contrast, generous margins, subtle paper-grain texture, a little flair — modern,
> confident, and classroom-friendly.

**ACE, in one line:** *Articulate* an idea in your own words → *Connect* it to evidence
and to other ideas → *Extend* it to a new text or situation.

## ⭐ Featured Title Poster — Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (Mark Twain)

A large **title-card / "cover" poster** for the Twain room — the dramatic centerpiece for
a wall or a room's landing. Uses the room's signature **river amber-gold** over the
PlotPoint base. *(Pairs with the PlotPoint **huckfinn** room.)*

> for **"Adventures of Huckleberry Finn" by Mark Twain**. **Core palette:** deep navy

> `#F5F4FB` text; **signature accent: Mississippi river-amber `#A06A2C` / warm gold-green

> quietly contrasting the **free river** with the **cruel shore**. Original instructional

> *"Down the river toward freedom — and against everything he was taught."*

> society," "Voice: first-person vernacular," "Theme: conscience vs. the crowd; satire of a

> keep imagery dignified and historically respectful — no caricature, no offensive text.

- **A:** Explain why the river feels free while the shore is dangerous. **C:** Connect Huck's choice to tear up the letter to what his society taught him was "right." **E:** When has doing right meant going against the crowd?

## Notes for the designer / teacher

- **Consistency:** keep the navy→plum→gold PlotPoint base on every poster so a wall of them
  reads as one family; let each **signature accent** provide the per-text pop.
- **No copyrighted art or long quotes:** all central images are original symbolic
  illustrations; callouts paraphrase themes/devices rather than quoting the text.
- **Adopt-locally:** grade placements follow common Texas ELAR practice (Grades 6–8; English
  I–IV = Grades 9–12). Confirm each title against your campus's adopted texts and community
  standards before printing — some titles vary by district and some are frequently challenged.
- **Pair & extend:** where a poster notes *Pairs with* a PlotPoint room, print the poster and
  assign the matching room so students move from **seeing** the analysis to **doing** it (ACE).
