Thinking routine

The ACE Framework

Every PlotPoint room runs on ACE — a simple Articulate · Connect · Extend routine, with the SOLO Taxonomy and optional, accountable Gen AI use built in. You can use it with any text, in any classroom.

What ACE is

ACE is a three-stage thinking routine that moves students from surface understanding to deep transfer. It is not three separate skills but one path: you Articulate an idea, Connect it to other ideas, then Extend it to something new. Because the stages are the same every time, students learn the moves once and reuse them with any story, poem, article, or primary source.

ACE pairs naturally with the SOLO Taxonomy (Structure of Observed Learning Outcomes), which describes how understanding deepens — from one idea, to several ideas, to connected ideas, to ideas that transfer.

The three stages

A · Articulate

Explain it in your own words

Goal: put one idea into your own words.
  • Identify, define, describe, or summarize
  • Name one relevant idea — or list several
  • Explain a term using an example
SOLO: Uni-structural → Multi-structural (surface understanding)
C · Connect

Show how ideas fit together

Goal: link ideas and explain why it matters.
  • Connect character & conflict, symbol & theme, cause & effect
  • Compare perspectives; explain how evidence supports a reading
  • Link an author's choices to their effect on the reader
SOLO: Relational (ideas connected and explained)
E · Extend

Apply it in a new situation

Goal: transfer the learning somewhere new.
  • Transfer a theme to another situation or text
  • Test an interpretation; offer a new perspective
  • Name where a comparison stops working
SOLO: Extended abstract (ideas that transfer)

How ACE maps to SOLO — and to Gen AI

As understanding deepens, the ACE stage advances — and the appropriate role for an optional AI tool changes with it. Think first; use Gen AI after effort, never before it.

Use ACE with any text

ACE is not tied to any one book. Here is a simple protocol you can run with any reading — a novel, a short story, a poem, an article, or a primary source.

  1. Read and think first. Students read (or listen) and jot a first impression before any tool or discussion.
  2. Articulate. Each student explains one key idea in their own words, with one detail from the text as support.
  3. Connect. Students link that idea to another idea, moment, or choice in the text — and explain why the connection matters.
  4. Extend. Students apply the idea to a new text or a real situation, and name where the comparison holds and where it breaks down.
  5. Reflect. If Gen AI was used, students complete a thinking receipt so the reasoning stays theirs.

Reusable ACE prompts (any text)

Copy these into any lesson. Replace the bracketed parts with your text and topic.

Articulate
Explain in your own words [one key idea] from [the text]. Include one detail from the text that helped you understand it.
Connect
Connect [idea one] to [idea two] in [the text]. Explain how putting them together deepens your understanding of a character, a theme, or the author's purpose.
Extend
Apply [the idea] to [a new text or real situation]. Explain what transfers, what changes, and where the comparison stops working.
ACE reflection
Articulate the text's central idea in your own words. Connect two moments that develop it. Extend it to the present, and name where the comparison becomes hard to hold.

Keeping Gen AI accountable

The rule

Think first. Use Gen AI after effort, not before it. Never let Gen AI replace your reasoning.

In PlotPoint, Gen AI supports are off by default and enabled only by a teacher, on that device. When used, students complete a thinking receipt — recording their first explanation and evidence, what they asked, what they accepted, changed, or rejected, and why — so the thinking stays their own. A non-AI alternative (trading receipts with a partner) is always offered. Nothing students write is ever transmitted; it stays in their browser.
