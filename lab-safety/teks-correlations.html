TEKS & Standards Alignment

Every module in the Lab Safety Certification maps to the 2021-adopted Texas science TEKS (implemented 2024–25), the statutes behind them, and supports for emergent bilingual learners and universal access.

01 · TEKS

Science TEKS — the safety expectation

In the current TEKS, lab safety lives in Strand 1, Scientific and Engineering Practices. The same student expectation repeats at every grade and course. Its full text: “use appropriate safety equipment and practices during laboratory, classroom, and field investigations as outlined in Texas Education Agency-approved safety standards.”

Grade / CourseTEKS CodeSafety focus at this level
Grade 6S.6.1.CIdentify and use everyday safety equipment and practices; follow the safety contract.
Grade 7S.7.1.CApply safe practices across more independent investigations; recognize hazard signs.
Grade 8S.8.1.CSelect appropriate PPE and emergency equipment for a given investigation.
BiologyBIO.1.CBiological/biohazard handling, disposal, and aseptic technique context.
ChemistryCHEM.1.CSDS interpretation, GHS pictograms, chemical compatibility and storage.
PhysicsPHY.1.CElectrical, mechanical, thermal, and optical (laser) safety in investigations.
IPCIPC.1.CCombined physical/chemical hazards; SDS hazard classes (flammable, corrosive, reactive).
Source: Texas Education Agency / TEKS Guide (19 TAC §§112.25–112.28 middle school; §§112.41–112.51 high school), 2021 adopted, implemented 2024–25.
02 · Law

The statutes and rules behind “TEA-approved safety standards”

The TEKS point to outside authority for the specifics. These are the sources that define what “appropriate safety equipment and practices” actually means in a Texas classroom.

AuthorityWhat it requires
TEC §38.005Each teacher and student must wear industrial-quality eye-protective devices (Z87+) in appropriate situations as determined by district policy.
TEA Safety Standards K–12The Charles A. Dana Center guide (funded by TEA) — the “TEA-approved safety standards” the TEKS reference. Covers PPE, emergency equipment, chemical safety, facilities, and training.
19 TAC Ch. 112The science TEKS themselves, including the requirement that 40% of instructional time be hands-on lab and field investigation.
19 TAC Ch. 61Facility and space standards for science classrooms and laboratories (overcrowding affects safety).
19 TAC Ch. 247Educators’ Code of Ethics — duty to comply with safety laws, rules, and policies.
Sources: Texas Education Code Ch. 38; Texas Administrative Code Title 19; TEA Texas Safety Standards: Kindergarten through Grade 12.

05 · UDL

UDL — Universal Design for Learning (CAST)

Each module is built on the three UDL principles so more learners can access, engage with, and demonstrate the content.

PrincipleIn the modules
Multiple means of RepresentationIdeas shown as text, icon, color, and video; hazards named in two languages.
Multiple means of Action & ExpressionStudents respond by sorting, sequencing, selecting, and typing — not one fixed format.
Multiple means of EngagementShort goals, immediate “prove-it” feedback, and a visible badge sustain effort.

07 · Access

Designed for every learner

UDL — Universal Design for Learning

Every module offers more than one way in, following the three CAST UDL principles:

  • Representation: text, icons, color, and video for each idea
  • Action & Expression: click, sort, sequence, and type responses
  • Engagement: short goals, instant feedback, a visible badge to earn

ELPS — Emergent Bilingual Support

Supports for English learners across all four language domains:

  • Full seven-language switch
  • Visuals paired with every key term
  • Sentence stems for written and spoken responses
  • A bilingual safety word bank in each module
