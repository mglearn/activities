# Barrier, Blind-Build, and Communication Activities

## General Activity Structure

For most activities:

1. Place partners or teams on opposite sides of a barrier.
2. Give both sides identical materials.
3. Let only the Describer see the completed model, image, pattern, or route.
4. Require the Describer to communicate using words only.
5. Allow or prohibit questions, depending on the round.
6. Reveal both results and compare them.
7. Discuss which words, questions, and communication strategies worked.

Suggested roles:

- Describer or Architect
- Builder or Creator
- Clarifier
- Messenger
- Observer
- Accuracy Checker

# Building and Construction Activities

## 1. Hidden Cup Structure

Materials: Two identical sets of cups and a cardboard screen.

Build a structure using four to eight cups. One student describes it while a partner recreates it without seeing the original. Compare cup position, orientation, spacing, and order.

Skills: Spatial vocabulary, sequencing, listening, clarification

## 2. Cup Structure Relay

Materials: Cups, two barriers, and open classroom space.

The Architect views the cup model. A Messenger travels to the Builder and repeats the instructions. The Messenger may not see the Builder's work.

Skills: Information retention, concise speaking, teamwork

## 3. One-Message Cup Challenge

Materials: Matching cup sets and a barrier.

The Describer studies the model and may give only one uninterrupted set of directions. The Builder cannot ask questions until the description is complete.

Skills: Planning, procedural language, working memory

## 4. Question-Limited Cup Challenge

Materials: Matching cup sets and question tokens.

The Builder receives three question tokens. Each clarifying question costs one token.

Skills: Strategic questioning, listening for missing information

## 5. Cup Coordinate Build

Materials: Cups and grid mats.

Students describe each cup using grid coordinates, orientation, and relationship to neighboring cups.

Example: "Place an upside-down cup at B3. Put an upright cup directly above it."

Skills: Coordinates, positional language, precision

## 6. Cup Tower Troubleshooting

Materials: Two matching cup structures, one containing an error.

The Describer knows the intended design. The Builder receives a nearly correct structure and must follow directions to find and repair the mistake.

Skills: Error analysis, comparison, corrective feedback

## 7. LEGO Copy Build

Materials: Two identical LEGO or building-block sets.

One student describes a hidden model by naming each brick's color, size, orientation, and attachment point.

Skills: Attributes, spatial reasoning, technical communication

## 8. Architect–Messenger–Builder

Materials: Building blocks, barriers, and role cards.

The Architect sees the structure. The Messenger carries spoken information to the Builder but may not see either model while receiving or delivering directions.

Skills: Communication systems, memory, teamwork

## 9. Silent Architect

Materials: Blocks, paper, and pencils.

The Architect cannot speak. The Architect writes directions for a Builder who cannot see the model.

Skills: Procedural writing, editing, precision

## 10. Blueprint to Building

Materials: Blocks and a simple blueprint or diagram.

One student sees a top, front, or side-view drawing and describes it so the Builder can produce the three-dimensional model.

Skills: Visualizing three-dimensional objects, perspective, geometry

## 11. Straw-and-Connector Structure

Materials: Straws and reusable connectors or modeling clay.

Partners recreate a hidden geometric structure using spoken descriptions of lengths, angles, vertices, and connections.

Skills: Geometry vocabulary, angles, three-dimensional reasoning

## 12. Pattern-Block Picture

Materials: Identical sets of pattern blocks.

One partner creates or views a pattern-block image and explains how to reproduce it.

Skills: Shape attributes, transformations, symmetry

## 13. Tangram Talk

Materials: Two identical tangram sets and barriers.

The Describer explains where and how to place each tangram piece without naming the finished image.

Skills: Rotation, reflection, orientation, geometry

## 14. Magnetic-Tile Model

Materials: Matching magnetic-tile sets.

Students describe a hidden flat or three-dimensional magnetic-tile model.

Skills: Shape vocabulary, faces, edges, spatial relationships

## 15. Index-Card Construction

Materials: Index cards, clips, and tape.

The Architect views a structure made from folded or connected cards. The Builder recreates it from verbal instructions.

Skills: Engineering communication, sequencing, structural reasoning

## 16. Classroom-Materials Machine

Materials: Pencils, erasers, rulers, clips, cups, and paper.

Create an abstract "machine" from common classroom objects. A partner must reproduce the arrangement.

Skills: Object attributes, relative position, detailed description

# Drawing and Visual Communication Activities

## 17. Back-to-Back Drawing

Materials: Simple line drawings, paper, and pencils.

Partners sit back-to-back. One describes a picture while the other draws it without seeing the original.

Skills: Descriptive language, listening, visualization

## 18. One-Way Versus Two-Way Drawing

Materials: Two comparable drawings.

Round one prohibits questions. Round two permits clarifying questions. Students compare accuracy between rounds.

Skills: Feedback, questioning, communication analysis

## 19. Shape-Only Drawing

Materials: Geometric drawings and paper.

The Describer may use only shape, size, line, angle, and position words. The Describer cannot name familiar objects.

Skills: Geometry vocabulary, precise language

## 20. Mystery Robot

Materials: Robot drawings composed of simple shapes.

One student describes a robot's head, body, controls, arms, and features. The partner draws it.

Skills: Attributes, symmetry, spatial relationships

## 21. Monster Match

Materials: Teacher-created monster cards.

Students describe a fictional creature using number, size, color, shape, and position vocabulary.

Skills: Adjectives, body-part vocabulary, listening

## 22. Describe the Flag

Materials: Fictional flag designs, paper, rulers, and colored pencils.

The Describer explains shapes, colors, proportions, and placement. The partner draws the flag.

Skills: Fractions, proportions, geometry, color vocabulary

## 23. Hidden Emoji Design

Materials: Teacher-created simple face or symbol cards.

Students describe the location and shape of eyes, mouth, eyebrows, and other features without naming the emotion.

Skills: Facial-feature vocabulary, inference, position

## 24. Pixel-Art Dictation

Materials: Blank grid paper and a completed pixel design.

The Describer gives coordinate and color instructions one square or region at a time.

Skills: Coordinates, coding concepts, accuracy

## 25. Symmetry Drawing

Materials: Half-completed symmetrical designs.

One student sees the complete design and directs a partner in completing the missing half.

Skills: Reflection, symmetry, measurement

## 26. Logo Without the Name

Materials: Simple, noncommercial symbol designs.

The Describer explains a symbol without naming what it represents. The partner draws it.

Skills: Shape description, abstraction, visual communication

## 27. Diagram Dictation

Materials: A simple science, math, or social studies diagram.

One student describes a labeled or unlabeled diagram. The partner recreates its structure and relationships.

Skills: Academic vocabulary, diagram interpretation

## 28. Drawing from Written Directions

Materials: Written direction cards.

Students follow written instructions to draw an image, then compare their drawings and revise unclear directions.

Skills: Procedural writing, editing, interpretation

# Arrangement and Scene Activities

## 29. Desk-Object Arrangement

Materials: Identical sets of five to eight classroom objects.

One student arranges the objects and explains how a partner should create the same arrangement.

Skills: Prepositions, orientation, relative position

## 30. Miniature Classroom Scene

Materials: Small furniture cutouts or manipulatives and room mats.

Students describe where to place desks, tables, doors, shelves, and people.

Skills: Map language, location words, classroom vocabulary

## 31. Playground Designer

Materials: Playground mats and matching picture pieces.

One student creates a playground scene and directs a partner to place slides, trees, benches, paths, and play areas.

Skills: Location, planning, community vocabulary

## 32. Eco-Friendly Schoolyard

Materials: Schoolyard mats and identical environmental feature cards.

The Describer places trees, recycling bins, gardens, water stations, and habitat features, then explains their locations.

Skills: Environmental vocabulary, spatial language, cause and effect

## 33. Dress the Character

Materials: Matching paper characters and clothing pieces.

One student dresses a character and explains the clothing choices and locations to a partner.

Skills: Clothing vocabulary, sequencing, attributes

## 34. Build a Face

Materials: Matching face mats and facial-feature pieces.

The Describer creates a face and guides a partner in matching the features.

Skills: Body vocabulary, emotions, positional language

## 35. Create a Habitat

Materials: Habitat backgrounds and animal, plant, water, shelter, and food pieces.

One student constructs a habitat scene. The partner recreates it while discussing where organisms belong.

Skills: Habitat vocabulary, ecological relationships, position

## 36. Historical Scene Builder

Materials: Background mats and historically appropriate people, buildings, and object cards.

The Describer arranges a historical scene and directs a partner to duplicate it.

Skills: Historical vocabulary, setting, evidence-based description

## 37. Story Scene Match

Materials: Story setting mats and character or object pieces.

Students create and copy a scene from a familiar story. Afterward, they narrate what might be happening.

Skills: Oral language, story elements, sequencing

## 38. Solar-System Placement

Materials: Matching space mats and planet cards.

The Describer gives directions for arranging planets, moons, spacecraft, or orbital paths.

Skills: Sequence, relative distance, science vocabulary

## 39. Food-Plate Arrangement

Materials: Paper plates and matching food cards.

One student creates a meal or nutrition plate and directs a partner in duplicating it.

Skills: Food groups, fractions, positional vocabulary

## 40. Community Map Builder

Materials: Blank street maps and matching building cards.

The Describer places a school, hospital, fire station, park, library, and stores. The partner matches the arrangement.

Skills: Community vocabulary, maps, location

# Patterns, Sequences, and Classification Activities

## 41. Bead-Pattern Barrier

Materials: Matching beads, strings, and a barrier.

One student creates a color, shape, or size sequence and describes it for a partner to reproduce.

Skills: Patterns, ordinal language, attributes

## 42. Linking-Cube Train

Materials: Matching colored cubes.

The Describer explains a cube train using color, order, and position.

Skills: Sequencing, patterns, ordinal numbers

## 43. Pegboard Pattern

Materials: Matching pegboards and colored pegs.

One student produces a peg pattern. The partner recreates it using row, column, color, and position instructions.

Skills: Coordinates, visual patterns, precision

## 44. Number-Sequence Build

Materials: Number cards.

The Describer arranges numbers according to a rule and explains the sequence without stating the rule directly. The partner recreates it and identifies the pattern.

Skills: Number patterns, mathematical reasoning

## 45. Fraction-Tile Arrangement

Materials: Matching fraction tiles.

One student arranges equivalent or combined fractions and explains the placement and relationships.

Skills: Fraction vocabulary, equivalence, comparison

## 46. Base-Ten Model Match

Materials: Base-ten blocks and place-value mats.

The Describer builds a number and explains the hundreds, tens, and ones arrangement.

Skills: Place value, mathematical language

## 47. Sort-It-the-Same

Materials: Identical collections of objects or cards.

One student sorts items into groups and describes the sorting rule so a partner can create the same groups.

Skills: Classification, attributes, reasoning

## 48. Mystery Sorting Rule

Materials: Matching sets of word, image, shape, or number cards.

The Describer explains where each item belongs but cannot state the category labels. The partner infers the rule.

Skills: Inductive reasoning, classification, vocabulary

## 49. Musical Instrument Sequence

Materials: Matching instrument cards or small percussion instruments.

One student arranges instruments in a sequence and describes the order. Students then perform the sequence.

Skills: Sequence, instrument vocabulary, listening

## 50. Rhythm Replication

Materials: Rhythm cards or percussion instruments.

One student sees or performs a rhythm. A partner reproduces it from spoken counting or rhythmic syllables.

Skills: Rhythm, listening, pattern recognition

# Map and Direction Activities

## 51. Route on a Map

Materials: Identical maps and pencils.

One student draws a route and describes it. The partner follows the directions and draws the same path.

Skills: Cardinal directions, landmarks, sequencing

## 52. Treasure-Map Directions

Materials: Matching grid maps with obstacles and landmarks.

The Describer guides a partner from the starting point to hidden treasure without revealing the destination.

Skills: Coordinates, direction, problem-solving

## 53. Coordinate-Plane Journey

Materials: Coordinate grids.

The Describer names ordered pairs or movement instructions. The partner plots and connects the points.

Skills: Ordered pairs, coordinate geometry, accuracy

## 54. School Navigation Challenge

Materials: Simplified school maps.

One student directs a partner from one school location to another using hallways, turns, and landmarks.

Skills: Functional directions, spatial awareness

## 55. Cardinal-Direction Builder

Materials: Compass mats and object cards.

Students place objects north, south, east, west, northeast, northwest, southeast, or southwest of a central location.

Skills: Cardinal and intermediate directions

## 56. Landform Map Match

Materials: Blank maps and landform symbols.

The Describer places mountains, rivers, lakes, valleys, and plains. The partner duplicates the map.

Skills: Geography vocabulary, map symbols, relative location

## 57. Grid Robot Programmer

Materials: Grid mats, tokens, and obstacle cards.

The Programmer gives step-by-step commands to move a token through the grid. The Operator follows the commands exactly.

Skills: Algorithms, sequencing, debugging

## 58. Human Robot Course

Materials: Floor grid or safe obstacle course.

One student acts as a robot and follows precise commands such as move forward, rotate, stop, and pick up.

Skills: Computational thinking, precise instructions, debugging

# Language and Literacy Activities

## 59. Vocabulary Object Match

Materials: Two identical sets of curriculum-related objects or cards.

The Describer explains an item without saying its name. The Listener identifies the matching item.

Skills: Definitions, attributes, academic vocabulary

## 60. Forbidden-Word Description

Materials: Vocabulary cards with prohibited clue words.

Students describe a term without using the term or several obvious related words.

Skills: Paraphrasing, vocabulary depth, flexible thinking

## 61. Character Description Match

Materials: Sets of character cards with similar features.

One student describes a character. The partner selects the exact matching card.

Skills: Adjectives, evidence, attention to detail

## 62. Which Picture Am I Describing?

Materials: Closely related image sets.

The Speaker describes one image while the Listener identifies it from several similar choices.

Skills: Distinguishing details, comparison, listening

## 63. Sentence-Building Barrier

Materials: Matching word or phrase cards.

One student constructs a sentence and describes the card order and punctuation to a partner.

Skills: Syntax, grammar, sentence structure

## 64. Story-Sequence Match

Materials: Matching sets of story-event cards.

One student arranges events and explains their order. The partner recreates the sequence.

Skills: Chronology, transition words, narrative structure

## 65. Comic-Strip Reconstruction

Materials: Matching comic panels.

The Describer arranges panels and explains the sequence without showing the images.

Skills: Cause and effect, sequencing, inference

## 66. Main-Idea Organizer

Materials: Matching idea and detail cards.

One student arranges a main idea with supporting details. The partner recreates the organizer from the explanation.

Skills: Main idea, supporting evidence, organization

## 67. Word-Web Replication

Materials: Vocabulary cards or blank concept-map templates.

The Describer explains a concept map, including which ideas connect and how.

Skills: Relationships, categorization, academic language

## 68. Oral-to-Written Procedure

Materials: A simple completed construction or drawing.

One student gives oral directions. The Listener writes them rather than completing the task. A third student later follows the written directions.

Skills: Speaking, note-taking, procedural writing

# Team Communication Variations

## 91. No-Questions Round

The Listener may not interrupt or ask for clarification.

Purpose: Demonstrate the limits of one-way communication

## 92. Questions-Allowed Round

The Listener may ask as many clarifying questions as needed.

Purpose: Practice feedback and confirmation

## 93. Yes-or-No Questions Only

The Listener may ask questions, but the Describer can answer only yes or no.

Purpose: Improve strategic questioning

## 94. Thirty-Word Limit

The Describer may use no more than thirty words.

Purpose: Encourage concise and prioritized directions

## 95. One Direction at a Time

The Builder must complete and confirm each step before receiving the next direction.

Purpose: Practice pacing and checking for understanding

## 96. Memory Messenger

The Messenger cannot take notes and may carry only one message per trip.

Purpose: Build working memory and message clarity

## 97. Written Messenger

The Architect writes each message. The Messenger delivers it without explanation.

Purpose: Compare written and oral communication

## 98. Multiple Messengers

Information passes through two or three students before reaching the Builder.

Purpose: Examine how messages change during transmission

## 99. Competing Voices

Two students provide directions, but only one has accurate information.

Purpose: Evaluate sources, seek confirmation, and manage conflicting information

## 100. Communication-Code Challenge

Teams receive planning time to create abbreviations, coordinates, labels, or shared terms before seeing the structure.

Purpose: Develop communication systems and shared vocabulary

## 101. Accuracy Before Speed

Teams receive points for exact matches and lose points for finishing with errors.

Purpose: Shift attention from speed to quality

## 102. Speed Before Accuracy

Teams try to finish quickly, then calculate an accuracy score.

Purpose: Discuss tradeoffs between efficiency and precision

## 103. Observer Feedback Round

An Observer records examples of clear language, vague directions, questions, and corrections.

Purpose: Make communication behaviors visible

## 104. Teach-Back Rule

After each direction, the Builder must restate it before acting.

Purpose: Check understanding and prevent assumptions

## 105. Error-Recovery Challenge

The teacher secretly moves or replaces one piece during the activity. The team must detect and repair the discrepancy.

Purpose: Troubleshooting, resilience, and collaborative correction

## 106. Student-Designed Challenge

Students create models, drawings, maps, or patterns for other groups to reproduce.

Purpose: Apply understanding by designing clear but challenging tasks

# Suggested Reflection Questions

1. Which directions produced the most accurate results?
2. Which words were too vague?
3. What information did the Describer assume the Listener already knew?
4. Which clarifying question helped most?
5. Did the group establish a shared starting point?
6. Was the information given in a logical order?
7. How did the Listener confirm understanding?
8. Where was information changed, lost, or misunderstood?
9. Did speed improve or reduce accuracy?
10. What communication rule would you change before another round?
11. How could diagrams, coordinates, labels, or shared vocabulary improve the task?
12. Where might these communication skills be useful outside this activity?
